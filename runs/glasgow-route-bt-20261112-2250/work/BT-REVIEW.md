# bt – review after routing the Glasgow board

I used `bt` for almost everything except deciding where things go. Roughly half of the copper on the board (the BGA fan-outs, LVDS fan, FX2 bus,
level-shifter block, Z lines, power links) was generated by my own small Python scripts as `items.json` and then checked and written with
`bt probe` / `bt apply`; the other half was `bt route`.

## What I used (approximate counts)
| Command | Times | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `bt apply` (incl. `removeAt`) | ~130 | essential, reliable |
| `bt route` single / `--batch` / `--nets` | ~100 calls | mixed, see below |
| `bt probe` (file and `--via`) | ~80 | the most valuable command |
| `bt ratsnest` | ~60 | essential; caught two router lies |
| `bt unroute` | ~35 | useful, two traps |
| `bt query` | ~30 | very useful |
| `bt render` | ~30 | very useful |
| `bt fill` | ~25 | fine, one side effect |
| `bt drc --baseline` | ~20 | excellent |
| `bt zone` | ~15 | fine; no way to delete/replace a zone |
| `bt part`, `bt board` | ~10 | good start-up overview |
| `bt plane` | 6 | good for power pins in open areas, bad next to fine-pitch pins |
| `bt length`, `bt same` | ~10 | fine |
| `bt pair` | 14 pairs, result discarded | |
| `bt escape`, `bt stitch`, `bt congestion` | 1–3 each | |
| `bt bus`, `bt place`, `bt near`, `bt islands`, `bt free`, `bt gap`, `bt cleanup`, `bt outline` | 0 | not needed / did not trust (cleanup) |

## What helped most
- **`bt probe`**: I could write geometry from a plan (a 45° fan, a repeated cell) and know in a second whether it was legal, with the offending
  item and the gap named. A 200–300 item script typically came back with 0–15 problems, each fixable from the message. Without it I would not
  have attempted the hand-planned parts at all. This saved more mistakes than anything else.
- **`bt drc --baseline`**: shows only what I added on a board with 400+ pre-existing warnings. First full run: 22 errors/15 warnings, all real.
- **`bt apply` with `removeAt` by position**: made surgical edits possible without touching the project file.
- **`bt ratsnest` counting zone fills**: this is how I found islands in my own In2 plane design (twice) and the router's unfinished links.
- **`bt route --batch` with explicit from/to, one layer, `maxVias:0`** in open areas (resistor arrays to shifter ports, short power links):
  fast (≈0.1–1 s each) and clean. `--through` waypoints worked well for the long edge runs (ENVA, ALERT) once the start was not boxed in.
- **`bt render`**: labelled pictures were enough to plan from; I never missed a GUI for looking.

## What got in the way or was wrong
1. **`bt route --shove` moved other nets onto inner layers.** With `--layers F.Cu,B.Cu` given, the "moved" tracks of U2, U5, P4–P7 and both VIO_EN
   nets ended up partly on In1.Cu (the ground plane) and In2.Cu. The output only says `moved …: shoved (7.46 mm)`. I found it by listing inner-layer
   tracks at the very end. Shove must never use a layer that the call did not allow, and should say which layers it used.
2. **`bt route … --layers …,In2.Cu` reports "ok" for links that are not connected.** Twice (SDA to its via, SCL to an F.Cu track) the In2 path
   stopped 0.3–0.55 mm short of the via, or ended under a track on another layer. One call printed `200/200 routed` with a list of 0 mm segments.
   `bt ratsnest` afterwards showed the gap.
3. **Multi-net routing is greedy and gives up quickly in dense areas.** FX2–FPGA bus, 22 nets, F+B, `--passes 3`: 4 min 39 s, 12/22. Bank Z/P nets:
   29/32 with long loops, `--rip` did not finish the rest. Pocket north of the FX2: three attempts, LEDs with 2–4 vias. Each time a planar solution
   existed and I ended up scripting it. A 2-pin net 9 mm apart came back as 68 mm with 5 vias; two re-routes of 1–6 mm gaps came back as 27 and 42 mm.
   A length/via budget (`--max-length`, or "fail rather than exceed N × ratsnest length") would have saved me from accepting or having to spot these.
4. **`bt pair` follows the start spacing and takes the greedy path**, so one pair's horizontal run under the connector boxed in the next pair's pads
   ("start is boxed in"). It has no notion of a fan of pairs. I discarded all of it.
5. **`--all` stops at the first link that fails** and leaves the other links of the net unrouted; I had to list from/to pairs myself.
6. **Router cannot start from 0.1 mm neck tracks at 0.25 mm pitch with the default width** ("start is boxed in"); it works with `--width .1`.
   The message does not hint at the width.
7. **`bt plane` puts 0.6 mm vias directly at the ends of 0.5 mm-pitch QFN pins**, which blocks the neighbouring pins' escape. It also reported
   "31/31 pads to the plane" for pads that are not above their net's zone at all (the via then dangles in another net's plane). It should check that
   the via position is inside the zone it is meant to reach, and respect neighbouring pin escapes or use the smaller via.
8. **`bt probe --via x,y` uses the 0.6 mm class via** unless given a file; I spent a while believing no via fitted where a 0.45 mm one did.
   `bt escape … --via-in-pad` answered "no spot within 3 mm" for pads where a probed 0.45 mm in-pad via was fine. A `--via-size` on both would fix it.
9. **`bt probe` on a file prints BAD but I still applied twice** – my mistake, but `bt apply --checked` (probe first, refuse on BAD) would make the safe path the default.
   Related: probe does not take the file's own `removeAt` into account, so a change that replaces a track always shows one false BAD.
10. **`bt unroute --layer B.Cu` also removes the net's vias**, and `--box` removes vias in the box; both surprised me once. `bt unroute` of a whole
    net is the only undo for a router result, which also deletes hand-made parts of that net – I kept re-applying my JSON files. An "undo last apply" would help.
11. **`bt stitch` + `bt fill`**: two stitching vias sat where no GND copper was on the outer layers but +3V3 was on In2; after the fill (pcbnew) they
    had become +3V3 vias 0.04 mm from GND header pins. Stitch should only place where the via really lands in its own net on ≥2 layers, or write non-free vias.
12. **`bt fill` leaves a `.kicad_prl` only when I used pcbnew myself** – fine – but there is **no `bt zone --delete/--replace`**, so every island reshape
    meant a pcbnew script and restoring the `.kicad_pro` by hand. I reshaped islands four times.
13. One probe run reported `gap 0.125 < 0.2` for +1V2 items although the class clearance was 0.125; the same segment passed with `--seg`. I never found out why.
14. `--nets "Net-(D*,…"` : a glob inside a comma list is not expanded and is reported as a net that is "already connected".

## What I did myself instead, and why
- All fan-outs and buses (≈1500 segments): small generators (ball/pin coordinates → 45° fans with computed offsets). The router has no concept of
  "these N nets leave in this order and arrive in that order", which is the whole problem around a BGA.
- Zone editing and final statistics with `pcbnew` (delete zones, list inner-layer tracks, count via sizes).
- Independent final check with `kicad-cli pcb drc` (same result as `bt drc`).

## What I would change / add
- Shove/rip must respect `--layers`; report every layer and net touched; add `--max-detour`.
- Make "ok" mean electrically connected on the same layer or through a via; never emit 0-length segments.
- `bt fan`: ordered escape for one side of a BGA/QFN (row assignment, via sites on the interstitial grid, exit order given by the user) – the one
  tool that would have saved me the most time. A pair/bus router that takes an ordered list and builds a non-crossing fan.
- `bt zone --delete` / `--replace`, and `bt islands` working per layer for any poured net with the reason ("cut by track X on In2").
- `bt apply --checked`, `bt undo`, `--via-size` on probe/escape/plane/stitch, a per-call length/via budget.
- Keep: probe, drc --baseline, ratsnest, query, render, apply/removeAt – these are good as they are.
