# Routing notes – macropad-top

Board: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, routed for JLCPCB's standard process. Placement is untouched
(all 54 footprints verified identical in position, rotation and side to the original).

**State:** fully routed. KiCad 10.0.8 DRC (`kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all --schematic-parity --refill-zones`)
reports 0 errors, 0 unconnected items and 0 schematic-parity issues. The one remaining warning
(`lib_footprint_mismatch` on U103) was there before routing.
It has not been opened in the GUI (none available) – I checked it by DRC and by rendered plots only.

The routing was generated by Python scripts (`pcbnew` API) from hand-planned coordinates, not by an
autorouter. The scripts are in `routing-scripts/` for reference only; they use scratch paths and will
not run as-is.

## Approach

The board has two different halves, so the layers swap roles:

| Area | F.Cu | B.Cu |
|---|---|---|
| USB / regulator / RP2040 / flash (x < 118) | components and almost all signals | ground plane, plus a few power feeds |
| Key matrix (x > 118) | unbroken ground plane | key lines, LED power, LED data (all pads are on the back there) |

Both layers carry a GND pour over the whole board, tied together with 76 stitching vias plus a via
at practically every GND pad. Signals change layer in the 118–123 mm strip between the two halves.

## Numbers used

- Signal tracks 0.2 mm (0.25 mm on the back in the key area), clearance 0.2 mm everywhere – nothing
  needed to go below 0.2 mm, so I left the Default net class as it was.
- Power: VBUS 0.5–0.8 mm, +5V 0.5–0.8 mm, +3V3 0.4–0.5 mm, +5V_LED 0.5–0.6 mm, +1V1 0.2–0.4 mm.
- Vias 0.6/0.3 mm (156) and 0.8/0.4 mm (5, on VBUS and the bulk cap ground). Tented both sides (board setting, unchanged).
- Pours: 0.25 mm clearance, 0.25 mm minimum width, thermal reliefs on pads, isolated islands removed.

## Changes to rules / net classes

- `.kicad_pro`: added net classes `Power` (0.5 mm, 0.8/0.4 via) and `USB`, assigned by net name.
  They document intent for future edits; clearances are unchanged.
- `.kicad_dru`: added one rule – copper pours keep 0.3 mm from board edges and the LED cut-outs
  (the JLC minimum of 0.2 mm still applies to tracks).

## Decisions, by block

**USB.** D+/D- run 0.2 mm on top from the connector through the USBLC6 (flow-through, under its body)
to the 27 Ω resistors and into the MCU, about 10 mm in total. The connector's interleaved A6/A7/B6/B7
pads cannot all be joined on one layer: D+ is joined on top, the second D- pad uses two vias and a
3 mm hop on the back. VBUS leaves both connector pads through 0.8 mm vias and runs on the back under
the connector to the fuse (no top copper tracks under the shell).

**Power.** Fuse → bulk cap → 0.6 mm +5V bus feeding the LDO and the LED load switch. +3V3 leaves the LDO
output caps through two vias and runs on the back to the RP2040, where it feeds the decoupling caps
that are placed under the chip. Each IOVDD/USB_VDD pin drops straight through a via to its cap. +1V1
(regulator output pin 45 → pins 50 and 23) links C116/C117 on the back and crosses to C115 on a back
track north of the chip. The exposed pad has 5 ground vias into the back-side cap grounds, plus three
corner vias for the caps outside the pad.

**QSPI.** All six lines fan out west and south on top in pin order, which matches the flash pinout
with no crossings and no vias (14–25 mm, not length-matched – not needed at these lengths).
BOOTSEL hangs off the CS line through a 2.5 mm hop on the back.

**Crystal.** Short top-layer tracks; the crystal's two ground pads and the load-cap ground are tied
together and have their own via to the plane.

**Keys.** The six lines leave the top of the RP2040 as a bus along the north edge. KEY1–3 drop to the
back and run along the top edge to their switches; KEY4–6 run down the gap between the two halves on
top and cross to the back for the bottom row. The order swap needed between KEY2 and KEY3 is done at
their layer change.

**LEDs.** +5V_LED goes along the south edge on the back, then as one 0.6 mm run: bottom row
(west→east), up the east edge, top row (east→west). It is a single run rather than a loop because a
loop would have to cross the key lines or the data chain. Worst-case drop at the last LED is about
20 mV at 6 × 36 mA. Data follows the schematic chain order; every LED has its 100 nF cap directly on
its supply pad and a ground via beside it.

## Known weaknesses / not done

- **USB is not impedance-controlled.** 90 Ω differential is not achievable with sensible widths on a
  1.6 mm 2-layer board; I kept the pair short and tight instead. Fine for full-speed USB (12 Mbit/s),
  which is all the RP2040 does.
- **Ground under the RP2040 on the back is cut up** by the +3V3 ring, the +1V1 link and the cap pads.
  The exposed pad reaches the main back plane through one explicit 0.3 mm track plus the corner vias
  and top-side links, not through a wide copper area.
- **Back-side feeds cross under signals:** the +3V3 feed passes under the six QSPI tracks, the flash
  +3V3 spur (10 mm) passes under CS/SD1/SD2, a +5V track for the USBLC6 clamp pin crosses the USB
  area diagonally. All are narrow and cross roughly at right angles; I judged this acceptable on a
  1.6 mm board but it is not ideal.
- **LED_EN and LED_DIN_3V3 take a long detour** (~45 mm, around the flash and along the south edge)
  because the pinout forces them through the QSPI area. Both are slow signals. They run on top
  close to or directly above the LED_DIN and +5V_LED tracks on the back for roughly 8–10 mm.
- **Vias touching pads:** several vias overlap the edge of back-side decoupling cap pads under the
  RP2040, and the 5 thermal-pad vias are open 0.3 mm holes (not plugged). Some solder wicking is
  possible on assembly; this is common practice on RP2040 boards but worth knowing.
- Stitching vias were placed automatically wherever both pours are solid, so a few sit under the USB
  shell and the hot-swap sockets. They are tented.
- No teardrops. No Gerbers/drill files generated – export those from this board when ordering.
- D501's DOUT (end of the LED chain) is unconnected, as in the schematic.
