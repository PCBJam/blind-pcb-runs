# Using bt on the Mega 2560

I would use bt again for local routing, probing and sign-off. On this board its most valuable feature was the chain from a proposed polyline to a clearance report, a checked edit, and a KiCad result. For the global 100-pin MCU-to-header problem I used Freerouting locally and brought its new copper back to the original board.

## Usage on this job

Counts are approximate and include scratch boards, unsuccessful trials and readbacks.

| Commands | Frequency | What they contributed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `help`, `board`, `rules`, `part` | Several help and part lookups, one full board inventory, three rule edits plus readbacks | The board inventory supplied pin order, part values, special fiducial clearances, a back-copper label and the initial ratsnest in one read. It saved a custom netlist dump. The coordinate output was sufficient for drawing the USB routes. Class edits preserved unrelated project settings. |
| `route` with individual, batch and multi-net requests | Roughly twenty calls | The first six clock, analog-supply and UCAP nets took about two seconds. The initial 25-request power/return batch found 22 new paths and one existing connection in about 11 seconds. Later calls closed RESET, shortened LED routes and connected the new shield supply. I spent more time on the topology and the exceptions than on coordinate searches. |
| `probe`, `apply`, `query` | About fifteen standalone probes, more than twenty applications, several local queries | These caught incorrect USB bends, an overly tight staggered via pitch, two routes too near a mounting cutout and candidate rail widths that would collide with other copper. Testing a 16-channel fanout as a single items file caught interactions that testing channels separately would miss. UUIDs made selective replacement practical. |
| `plane`, `via --near`, `stitch`, `islands` | One plane pass, two nearby-via searches, one grid stitch pass, one island repair trial | The plane pass served 39 of 44 requested pads, with sharing. It identified why a drop from U3 pin 32 could not go straight into the back. The 8 mm stitch grid gave 18 immediately legal sites. The island trial correctly identified J12 pin 6 and the C18/U3 ground group. |
| `zone`, `fill`, `ratsnest` | About a dozen zone operations and several fills and connectivity checks | I could build four zones, vary thermal settings and compare fills on scratch boards. Reporting the pads in each disconnected ground group was considerably easier to act on than KiCad's zone-to-zone DRC marker. |
| `unroute`, `cleanup`, `length`, `vias` | Several selective removals and length checks, one cleanup preview, one final via-minimum check | These isolated individual LED and supply paths and exposed a 30.9 mm route between points only 5.1 mm apart. I used the cleanup proposal to understand overlapping imported segments. |
| `render`, `drc`, `check`, `same` | About a dozen renders, two bt DRC runs, one comprehensive check, two placement comparisons | Rendering made a visual log inexpensive. Baseline DRC distinguished the original 12 errors and 101 warnings from routing findings. The final check confirmed current fills, zero opens, zero split nets and zero additional bt findings. I also ran KiCad's CLI independently. |

## Where the result needed interpretation

- **Width and via limits.** `bt route --max-vias 5` on the long 5 V supply attempt found a nine-via path and then rejected it. Increasing via cost produced a seven-via option. Defining a back-edge corridor myself produced a cleaner distribution path. I would like the search to use the remaining via allowance as a constraint, or to report that it only filtered the best path found.
- **Global pours.** `bt stitch --net GND --pitch 8` skipped 51 grid sites because one of the saved ground fills was absent. Some of those areas would become useful copper if stitched. `bt islands --net GND --fix` proposed about 15 mm of track and a via for one component and found only an over-budget 31.58 mm option for J12. I retained floating copper on a scratch fill, found overlaps between the two faces, and checked candidate vias with bt. After refilling the production zones with island removal enabled, all pads connected. An option to plan stitches against provisional fill and verify the resulting islands would have saved this custom analysis.
- **Conservative clearance estimates.** Probing the Freerouting result flagged eight segments, including a 0.142 mm estimate at U3 near PE3 and a 0.147 mm estimate near TXD1. KiCad's all-track-errors DRC on that board reported no copper-clearance violations. I used the final KiCad result for acceptance. Identifying the approximation used for each obstacle would make discrepancies easier to investigate.
- **Connectivity summaries.** One `bt ratsnest` result started with “5 nets not fully connected” and printed only GND's four groups. A later run said four nets and again printed only GND. The listed ground pieces were actionable. I would like the headline to count the same entities as the detail, or show separate counts for disconnected pads, floating track ends and zone islands.
- **Exchange precision.** KiCad’s DSN and the session agree on electrical contact through copper width, but they can disagree on centerline endpoints by less than a micrometre. The imported session consequently added redundant, tiny track ends at some protected routes. `bt cleanup --tol .003 --stub .002` proposed removing 134 overlapping segments and splitting 27 tracks. I used the original track identities to discard redundant imported copper and split genuine T junctions while preserving the hand-selected widths. An import-aware cleanup that gives original or wider copper precedence would help here.
- **Completion status.** A batch can write useful copper and exit successfully with failed or skipped links listed in its summary. The partial results were worth keeping. A machine-readable disposition per request, available without parsing a log, would make retry and audit scripts easier.
- **Scope of placement checking.** `bt same` accurately describes its locked-placement check. Every footprint was fixed by the task, so I also compared all 66 serialized footprint blocks and board-level graphics against the original. An all-footprints option would cover this common requirement directly.

The local Freerouting run completed its bulk search and optimization in about 47 seconds with the critical copper protected. I did not benchmark a full-board bt-only run. The measured advantage of bt here was fast local iteration and useful failure locations. I did not measure token savings.
