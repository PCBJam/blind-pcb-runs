# Board-tools review

I used bt alongside KiCad 10.0.8, pcbnew, a local Specctra router and geometry scripts. Counts below are approximate where marked. I used KiCad's saved-board DRC as the authority for connectivity.

| Command | Use and result |
| --- | --- |
| `board`, `nets`, `part`, `query`, `rules` | Initial inventory and repeated local lookups. The single board dump had 532 lines and included the net endpoints. Saving it and searching by reference or signal was quicker than reading the whole dump repeatedly. The rules output made the 0.1499 mm clearance, via dimensions and pair-class membership explicit. |
| `render` | Most numbered progress pictures. The layer and window selectors made the dense FPGA breakout legible. I used KiCad SVG output to inspect the original board legends and mask, which were valuable placement guides. |
| `pair` | Two trials during USB placement. It found an initial path. After moving the ESD array, the end-to-end call failed. A manually constructed coupled path plus short branches cleared the same geometry. A report showing both the last viable pair cross section and its nearby obstacles would have simplified this. |
| `fan` | One trial on the FPGA. It enumerated 44 signal escapes and 11 plane pins. The proposal included nets with existing controlled routes, so I retained the existing copper and wrote a constrained escape search for the remaining balls. A switch to escape only specified unrouted physical pins would help. |
| `probe`, `apply --checked` | Repeated during pair routing and USB branching. The first expansion candidate had 24 conflicts in 225 proposed pieces. The final 234-piece proposal checked clear. Testing a whole bundle against itself before committing was one of the largest time savings. |
| `plane`, `zone`, `fill`, `islands` | Two principal pad-to-plane passes, three initial internal regions and later power-island investigation. Plane generation placed 162 ground vias and 36 supply vias in minutes. Some early escapes blocked nearby fine-pitch signals. The islands command correctly identified eight 3.3 V copper groups and named representative pins and vias. It found an explicit join for one of seven breaks. The other six needed layout changes. |
| `route` | Several small trials and two larger batches. An early batch connected three of twelve difficult backside FPGA escapes and produced long detours, so I discarded it. A later batch completed 47 of 121 selected nets in about 168 seconds and retained useful partial work. KiCad's open count fell from 202 to 131. Reporting completed physical connections alongside completed nets would make the batch result easier to interpret. |
| `same`, `length`, `ratsnest`, `drc` | Used for geometry preservation, pair-length inspection and connectivity checks. The locked-part comparison was especially valuable after Specctra import. Reports which distinguish whole-net length from driver-to-receiver length would help with the branched USB connector. |

I placed parts with pcbnew using the fixed legends, schematic groupings and courtyard shapes. For the dense breakout I needed access to individual ball positions, backside pads, candidate segments and neighboring escapes together. I used local geometric search and the KiCad connectivity graph to choose escapes, route around existing copper, and revisit individual obstructing sections. For this board I would add a pin-field planner which shows reachable layers after an escape via is placed. A via can meet local clearances yet be enclosed by neighboring vias on every useful routing layer.

## Finishing the release

- I ran `tune` once for each of the 13 expansion pairs and used `length` again on the resulting board. The largest starting mismatch was 2.873 mm. The saved pairs ended within 0.001 mm of each other in geometric P/N track length. The tool identified pours that needed refilling after the bumps. This finished the entire tuning pass without manually placing individual meanders.
- I ran `cleanup --dead` several times on isolated checkpoints. On the electrically complete board it brought the native dangling-track count from 78 to 32 on the adjusted-drill version. Some retained stubs met another item along their length. Native DRC also found new loose ends after the first dead branch disappeared. I removed those in verified rounds, then walked the remaining endpoint chains back to protected pads or junctions. The saved release has zero dangling tracks or vias. Repeating the native leaf check internally until it reaches a fixed point would save multiple refill-and-DRC cycles.
- I used `fill` throughout the repair and release trials, including after the impedance-tuning pass, and `render` for 18 numbered views. KiCad supplied the original legend inspection and final front/reverse render. The progress log contains 21 image-and-note pairs.
- I ran `same` at intermediate imports and on the release. Its final output reports all 243 unlocked parts moved, zero locked parts moved, no added or removed footprints and unchanged pad nets. I separately normalized footprint orientation and compared pad geometry and UUIDs because the preservation requirement mattered here.
- The initial under-minimum via check counted 113 legal 0.30/0.35 mm laser vias against the 0.40 mm mechanical-via floor. KiCad distinguishes microvia and mechanical-via process limits. I encoded the smallest fabricated via and drill as the global floor, with explicit 0.40/0.20 mm mechanical-via and 0.20 mm component-drill rules. The release then produced zero separate bt findings. Checking the actual drilling span before suggesting a resize would avoid a potentially destructive change to the HDI field.
- I ran `check` on scratch checkpoints and on the actual release. `bt check /work/glasgow.kicad_pcb` terminated in about 15 seconds with “Maximum call stack size exceeded.” The board file was byte-identical before and after that failure. `bt check /work/glasgow.kicad_pcb --no-fill` completed, reported **zero unconnected items, zero nets in pieces and zero bt findings**, and printed **CHECK FAILED**. It promoted the eleven inherited fixed-courtyard warnings to errors and counted four individually excluded assembly-type findings. It also reported the 421 inherited schematic-parity findings. Its DRC invocation uses KiCad's `--severity-all`, which includes exclusions. A separate line for excluded findings, a separate parity result and an iterative comparison for large fills would make this outcome much clearer.
- I verified the same saved board with KiCad's error-only DRC and exit-code checking. It returned zero errors and zero opens. A native refill produced the same polygon digest for every zone layer. A pin-by-pin schematic comparison and manufacturing exports supplied the remaining release checks.

For another fine-pitch board I would use the geometry probe, checked application, pair-length tuning and locked-footprint comparison again. The most expensive manual work was reconnecting islands whose DRC markers referred to whole zones, and protecting narrow ground paths while adding a different signal. Showing the actual disconnected filled polygon and allowing a repair to terminate on a named, physical ground pad or via would have shortened that work substantially.
