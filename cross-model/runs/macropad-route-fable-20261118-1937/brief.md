The KiCad 10 project in /work (board file: /work/macropad-top.kicad_pcb) has all footprints placed but no copper routing: no tracks, no vias, no zones.

Your job: route this board so it is ready to order from JLCPCB as a standard 2-layer board. Route it the way an experienced PCB designer would for a product they care about, not only so it passes DRC. Think about the circuit (the schematics are in /work) and decide your own approach: track widths, layer use, copper pours, via use, and so on.

Rules:
- Do not move, rotate, flip, add or remove footprints. The placement is fixed.
- You may add tracks, arcs, vias and zones, and you may edit the net classes and design rules if you have a reason to.
- Save the result in place as /work/macropad-top.kicad_pcb, readable by KiCad 10.
- When you are done, write /work/NOTES.md: your approach, the decisions you made and why, and anything you know is not finished or not good.

Tools: `kicad-cli` (KiCad 10.0.8) and the `pcbnew` Python module are installed. Python, Node and pip/npm work. You have internet access through a proxy, and some sites are blocked. There is no GUI.

Work on your own until the board is finished; nobody will answer questions.



Board tools: `bt` is installed (run `bt help`, which has examples; `bt help route` shows one command). It reads a .kicad_pcb and the rules next to it and answers in short text, without writing anything unless you ask. It keeps the same rules KiCad's DRC does for copper: net classes, edge clearance, pad and footprint clearance overrides, copper logos, and the .kicad_dru's clearance rules (`bt board` says which it applies). The tools:
- `bt board` prints the whole board in one go: rules, parts, every net with its pads, the ratsnest, copper text and solder-mask openings. `bt nets` is the netlist alone. `bt part`, `bt query` (with each track's and via's uuid), `bt gap`, `bt length`, `bt vias` (filter by net, size, drill, box, or what is under the board minimums) and `bt free` answer smaller questions. `bt rules` shows the net classes and board minimums, and sets them in the .kicad_pro (a class's widths, clearance, vias, pair sizes and nets; the minimums), changing nothing else in the file.
- `bt congestion` shows, before you route, where it will be tight; `bt islands` shows which pieces of a net no pour could join, with the track or via that joins each (`--fix` writes them); when a pour could reach every piece but the fill leaves some apart, it names them, says why (an old fill, thermal spokes, clearance) and gives a join for each.
- `bt route` routes one connection, a net's missing links, or several nets at once (`--nets`, shortest first, or `--order escapes-first`: the nets leaving a crowded part edge first, in pin order; with shove and rip-up passes for what fails). It takes keepout boxes per layer, no-via boxes, via sites, a narrower neck, a via limit and a length budget, and returns coordinates or names what blocks the route and where. With `--nets` it writes its output after every net (`--budget` caps the time), keeps the way out of fine-pitch pins free, and won't wall in an unrouted pad. `--shove`/`--rip` move earlier tracks out of the way, only on the layers you allowed, and undo everything if any moved net would end up less connected. With `--nets`, the shove and rip-up passes never move copper that was on the board before the call (`--move-board` allows it), `--fixed` takes names or patterns, every moved net is named, and a net still not connected at the end has its links taken out again. Routes stay off inner plane layers (`bt board` lists them) unless `--layers` names them, and say when they run through another net's pour; a boxed-in end is necked to 0.127 mm, the usual fab minimum (`--neck-min` for another), and necked routes are counted in the summary (`--max-neck-length` caps them). With `--via-sites`, `--end-vias N` lets a via next to either end be off the sites, and a failure says when the sites were the reason. A pad named in `--from`/`--to` on a poured net is reached on its own copper, a route far longer than the straight line is flagged as a detour, and a boxed-in pin's failure names the pins whose routes wall it in. `-o` writes only what the call added and took out, never the `--with` copper again; `bt merge` joins several items files into one, each segment once. `--progress FILE` keeps a long run's progress lines in a file. `bt bus` routes nets side by side (`--window MIN,MAX` or `--window D` checks their lengths, `--tune` bumps the short ones), `bt planar` says whether nets between two parts (or fanning out of one) can go on one layer, which pairs must cross and the fewest nets to move, `bt pair` a differential pair or a group of pairs side by side (`--tune` takes the skew out). `bt tune` adds length to routed nets with 45° bumps: a pair's skew, a bus matched, or a net brought to a set length.
- `bt fan` escapes a BGA or a fine-pitch IC side by side (rows between the balls on the top layer, dog-bone vias for the next rows) and lists where each net comes out, in order along each side; for a QFN/QFP side it also says which nets go to the second layer so that none cross, and gives power pins a stub and via; `--dogbone` takes every pin of a 0.35-0.5 mm QFN side down a via, in two staggered rows, and checks each via has a way on. `bt escape U3 --side S` plans a whole side of a QFN/QFP, power pins included: a stub and via per pin, staggered on two rows, with neighbouring pins of one net sharing a via, and a pair (or `--keep` nets) left on its layer with its way out kept free.
- `bt escape`, `bt plane` and `bt stitch` add stubs and vias (a `--via-size` under the board minimums is refused) to reach pours (keeping fine-pitch pins' ways out free; `--avoid` boxes; `--outside` keeps vias out of the part's own body; when no spot fits, it counts why: which box, whose way out, what copper); `bt via --near PAD` finds the nearest legal via spot and says why the spots next to the pad don't work; `bt route --net GND --from PAD` ends on the pour. When a zone's saved fill is older than the copper, bt says so: refill (`bt fill`) before trusting what the pours connect. A new zone takes its net class's clearance and joins SMD pads solid (thermal reliefs on through-hole pads only) unless you say otherwise.
- `bt offset --pair P,N --pts ...` draws a pair from its centre line, probed, with each half's length.
- `bt probe` checks proposed tracks and vias against the board and against each other (exit 1 on any problem); `--vias "x,y;x,y;..."` / `--segs` checks many candidates at once, each alone, and lists the clear ones; `bt apply --checked` probes first and writes nothing if anything is wrong.
- `bt place` tries placements and `--apply` writes them; without `--apply` it says "TRIAL ONLY, board unchanged" (each part's problems listed first, a problem a part already has where it is now not counted; a pad in an unrouted fine-pitch pin's way out is allowed, with a warning). `bt near` finds legal spots, clear of `--with` copper and of fine-pitch pins' ways out, and with no spot says why the spots were passed over (whose courtyard, whose way out, what copper); `bt near --each "C5:U103.1,C6:U103.10"` places several parts one after another, each next to its pin (`--apply` writes them). `bt outline` draws the board edge: a rectangle or polygon with rounded corners, tabs and notches, or `--fit` round what is placed; it also says where the back side has room.
- `bt render` draws a labelled PNG of any window, zone fills included.
- `bt drc --baseline` runs KiCad's DRC and shows only what is new, and says which checks the project switches off (`--courtyards` runs that one); it adds bt's own checks: vias and tracks under the board minimums, tracks under 0.127 mm, and track ends that meet their net only on another layer with no via (`bt ratsnest` names those too). With `--refill`, it also says (and exits 1) when the saved fills differ from a fresh fill, since KiCad on the saved file sees the old one. `bt check` does it all in one: fills in place, KiCad's DRC on the saved board with courtyards, what is unconnected, bt's own checks, and one CHECK OK / CHECK FAILED line. `bt same` checks that locked parts didn't move.
- `bt apply` (one items file or several, merged in order), `bt via`, `bt rm` (by point, one `--segment`, or `--uuid`; it and `bt unroute` say per net whether it comes apart, with `--dry` too), `bt seg` (move one segment's end, with what meets it there, or change its width; probed), `bt vias --set-size`, `bt unroute`, `bt cleanup` (dangling ends as KiCad's DRC sees them: dead branches of a connected net go, a T is split, and each loose end it keeps is named with the reason), `bt zone` (add, `--delete`, `--replace`, `--edit`, pad connection and islands; it fills), `bt fill`, `bt silk` (one reference by hand, or `--fix`: every reference on pads, silk or the edge moved to a clear spot round its part) and `bt outline` write to the board (copper as plain text; zones, fills and placements through pcbnew on a copy, with only the .kicad_pcb coming back). Each write keeps the old board: `bt undo` puts it back. With `-o` alone, the board is unchanged (bt says so), and with neither `-o` nor `--apply` it is a trial (bt says that too). A run over 10 s ends with a "bt CMD done" line on stderr and in the `--progress` file; with `--progress`, a run that is killed gets a line saying so there. A board changed by another writer during a run is not overwritten, and bt names that writer.

You decide the floorplan, the layer use and the order; the tools do the coordinates and the checks. Use them or not, as you see fit.

Good to know: saving a board through pcbnew can rewrite the .kicad_pro next to it with defaults. Keep a copy of it, or write with `bt`, which only touches the board file. bt reads the rules from the .kicad_pro next to the board: a copy of the board in another folder is checked against KiCad's defaults (bt says so).

Progress log: keep a visual log of how the board develops in /work/progress/. Each time you reach a step worth seeing (a placement attempt, a routing stage, a DRC round, a change of plan), save a numbered pair: a picture of the board (or of the area you worked on) as NNN-<short-name>.png and a short NNN-<short-name>.md with what you did, why, and what the result was. Number them in order (001, 002, ...). Keep the attempts that failed or that you threw away too, and say in their note why they failed. Aim for one entry every few minutes of work, not for every command.
