# RaspberryPiNVMeNAS – placement and routing notes

State of `/work/RaspberryPiNVMeNAS.kicad_pcb`: all 254 footprints placed, every net connected (0 unrouted),
KiCad 10 DRC shows nothing beyond what the untouched original already showed (17 `footprint_type_mismatch`
errors and text/library warnings that belong to the footprints themselves). Locked parts, including their
reference texts, are exactly where they were. Nothing was added to or removed from the netlist.

The scripts that generated the hand-designed copper are in `/work/scripts/`; the step-by-step pictures are in
`/work/progress/`.

## What the board is

CM4 carrier with an ASM1184e PCIe switch (U12) feeding four M.2 NVMe sockets, seven TPS563202 bucks
(5 V, 1.2 V, 3.3 VA, 4 × 3.3 V per SSD) behind a TPS2420 eFuse, an RP2040 for fans/thermistors/power
sequencing, Gigabit Ethernet and two USB-C ports.

## Placement

- **Height drove the big decision.** The whole right two-thirds of the top side is under the SSDs and the
  left-centre is under the CM4. I assumed 4.2 mm M.2 sockets (about 2.2 mm free under a single-sided SSD, under
  1 mm under a double-sided one) and that low parts under the CM4 are acceptable because the locked SD socket
  already sits there. So the six bucks that must live in those areas are on the **back** (2 mm inductors,
  1.25 mm 0805 caps), together with the LDO and the decoupling caps. Only 0402s, QFNs/DFNs and LEDs are on top
  under a module. 109 of 254 parts are on the back, so this is a double-sided assembly.
- **Power entry and the 5 V buck** are on top in the only top area not covered by anything (below the DC jack):
  jack → TVS → eFuse → 12 V.
- **Each M.2 buck sits on the back directly under its socket's 3.3 V pins**, output caps a few mm from the pins.
- **U12** is under socket 2, rotated so that its port order (3, 2, 1, 4 from west to east, upstream to the
  south-west) matches the socket order and the CM4 – no pair bundle has to cross another. Its exact position
  (171.5, 87.5) is a compromise between the 45° run port 2 needs and the H10 standoff pad.
- **The two core bucks (1.2 V, 3.3 VA)** are on the back south of U12, clear of the top-layer pair lanes.
- **RP2040** sits next to its locked USB-C, boot and reset parts; crystal on its west side, flash on the east.
- **All 16 status LEDs are in one column on the free left edge** (the only edge strip not under a module),
  each with its resistor beside it. Order top to bottom: power good, fault, M.2 1–4, RP2040 1–4, ACT, CM4 1–4, PWR.
- TVS arrays in line between CM4 and RJ45 (flow-through), USB choke at J7, temperature sensor and I²C pull-ups
  at the Qwiic connector, rail test points on the back at each buck output.

## Layer use

| Layer | Use |
|---|---|
| F.Cu | parts, all long PCIe/USB/Ethernet pairs, short signals, GND fill |
| In1.Cu | solid GND, no routing (reference for everything on top) |
| In2.Cu | GND, with islands: +12V strip under the buck inputs and a branch to the core bucks, +1V2 under U12 and to its buck, +5V from the buck to the CM4 pins and fan M4, +3.3VSTB under the RP2040 |
| B.Cu | bucks and their pours, the short PCIe socket entries, slow signals, GND fill |

The islands on In2 were shaped so that the back-layer PCIe sections run over In2 ground, not over a power island.

## Routing

- **PCIe (15 pairs, 0.2/0.2 mm, generated by `scripts/cells.py` and `pairs.py`).** The switch pinout puts every
  port's clock on the wrong side of its data pairs and the data pairs mirrored P/N against the socket, so some
  crossing is unavoidable. It is solved once and repeated four times in a "socket cell": the clock stays on
  top and enters through the M-key notch of the socket; RX and TX drop to the back just south of the socket,
  swap P and N at their up-vias between the pad rows (both halves the same length by construction) and the TX
  coupling caps sit in those back-layer lanes. Everything else is on top over the solid plane. On the CM4 side
  the reference clock dives under the H10 standoff pad and CM4-TX hops under CM4-RX once. All pairs are matched
  within 0.02 mm; longest is port 4 at 82 mm.
- **Bucks:** one template for all seven – input cap straddling the SW trace right at VIN/GND, bootstrap link
  under the IC, a ground strip with vias on the capacitor side, output caps flanking the inductor.
- **M.2 3.3 V rails:** back-side pour from the buck to the west pin group and along the top board edge to the
  east pin group, so it never passes under the back-layer PCIe lanes.
- **U12 supply pins:** stub and via just inside the pin ring, decoupling caps radially under the ring on the
  back, 9 vias in the exposed pad. RP2040 the same way, with its 1.1 V rail linked between the pad vias.
- **Ethernet** 0.15/0.2 mm pairs straight through the TVS arrays; **USB** 0.2/0.2 mm with the two D− pads of
  each receptacle joined behind the pad row.
- **Everything slow** (LEDs, fans, thermistors, enables, I²C, SD) was auto-routed with `bt route` and then
  corrected by hand where it was wrong (see `progress/005` and `006`).

## Rule changes (in the .kicad_pro)

- Net classes added: PCIe, USB (0.2/0.2), ETH (0.15/0.2), Power (0.4 mm, 0.6/0.3 vias); Default track and
  clearance raised from 0.127 to 0.15 mm.
- Copper-to-edge clearance 0 → 0.3 mm (the pours would otherwise reach the routed edge).
- Minimum thermal spokes 2 → 1. Fourteen through-hole pads get only one spoke on one of their layers; each is
  still tied fully on the In1 plane. The +12P pour at the DC jack was made solid instead, because that pin has no other layer.

## Not finished / not good – please read

- **Heights are assumptions.** I could not check the M.2 socket height or the CM4 stacking height. If the
  CM4 uses the 1.5 mm connectors, the 0402s and TVS arrays under it need a look; under a double-sided SSD U12
  (0.9 mm) is tight.
- **Impedance is not verified.** Widths assume about 0.2 mm prepreg between the outer and inner layers
  (≈90 Ω for 0.2/0.2, ≈100 Ω for 0.15/0.2). Order with controlled impedance or adjust to the fab's stackup.
- **The auto-routed slow nets are long and via-heavy**: LED_V+ 189 mm/9 vias, PCIe_LED_4 184 mm/10 vias,
  TSENSE_2 162 mm/8 vias, VMON 99 mm/7 vias, the RP2040 LED nets 140–155 mm with 4–8 vias. They work, but a
  person would have bundled them. They also cut the back-side ground fill into many pieces (In1 is the real
  return plane; 202 stitching vias tie the fills to it).
- **Thermistor (TSENSE) and VMON/IMON lines are unshielded and long**; no check of what they pass apart
  from the buck switch nodes (nothing closer than 0.65 mm) and the PCIe pairs.
- **A few slow nets still run 0.38 mm beside a PCIe pair for 2.7–3.8 mm** (PERST# beside each port clock in the
  socket, PCIe_LED_1 beside the port-1 clock). `scripts/pair_neighbours.py` lists them.
- **Return vias at the PCIe layer changes are incomplete**: socket J2 has none beside its dive vias and J3 only
  one (other copper was in the way); they rely on the stitching vias a few mm away.
- **Buck feedback sense tracks were auto-routed** and not reviewed one by one; at least the +3.3V_M3 one takes
  a detour over the top layer. The 5 V sense was redone by hand from the output cap.
- **eFuse U3 has only one ground via in its exposed pad and one beside it** (back-layer tracks under it); the
  flash U17 pad is grounded through a top-layer track only.
- CM4_RUN is necked to 0.127 mm for 2 mm at the RP2040. R65 (thermistor pull-up) ended up on the back.
- Five short GND tracks from `bt islands --fix` lie inside the In1/In2 ground planes; harmless, but In1 is not
  literally track-free.
- In the hand-made copper via-in-pad was avoided except C17 (0805, +3V3), where a via sits in the pad. I did not
  audit every via the auto-router placed.
- Not done at all: fiducials and tooling holes (no footprints may be added), teardrops, silkscreen tidy-up
  (reference texts are 0.5 mm, below the board's own 0.8 mm text rule, as delivered), signal-integrity or
  thermal simulation, a 3D collision check.
- `bt check` crashed on the final board, so the final verification is `kicad-cli pcb drc` on the saved file.
