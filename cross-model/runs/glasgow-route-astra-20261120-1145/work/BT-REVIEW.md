# Using bt to route Glasgow

I would use bt again for fanout planning, local clearance probes, plane connections, inspection and checkpoint renders. The first bulk route completed 116 of 186 outstanding nets in 341 seconds. Closing the remaining congested connections and deciding which return paths to protect took the most engineering effort. I used KiCad as the final authority on connectivity and fabrication output.

## Where the output affected the design

- The VIOB plane pass reported “0/31 pads to the plane” and “every via is inside its pour there” on the same line. Its detailed failures correctly identified competing 3.3 V copper under prospective via sites. The VIOB polygon had lost its unanchored main island, leaving only 1.92 mm² of fill. Temporarily retaining islands let 30 of 31 pads acquire vias. Restoring island removal and refilling then kept the bank connected. Reporting retained area versus outline area and suggesting a first anchor would have shortened the diagnosis.
- At a requested 0.28 mm width, route identified the two positive USB segments boxing in VUSB. The blocker coordinates were specific enough to preserve the signal routing and take VUSB across the back with two local vias. In the large pass, the one-second search parameter allowed multi-link nets such as SCL to run for much longer. Separate per-link and per-net elapsed times in the progress output would make budgets easier to choose.
- The default final check on the roughly 4.8 MB filled board stopped with “Maximum call stack size exceeded”. Running the same bt entry point with a larger process stack and Node stack completed in 52 seconds. It reported CHECK FAILED with 72 errors, 346 warnings, zero opens, zero nets in pieces and zero bt findings. Of those errors, 68 are inherited courtyard overlaps which check promotes to errors, and four are documented KiCad footprint-type exclusions. Native project DRC reports zero unexcluded errors and 414 warnings. Reporting excluded items separately and showing the severity changes beside the verdict would make that result much easier to interpret.
- Length reports sum a net's copper, including test-point branches. For USB I measured the two J1-to-U1 paths separately. A pad-to-pad length mode, with the traversed vias and reference-plane coverage along that path, would have saved that calculation. Pair-aware BGA escape planning would also have reduced the hand work at J5.

## Work done outside bt

I drew the USB paths, expansion pair centre runs, mirrored shunt connections and supply polygons explicitly. For general routing I carried the short bt routes into Freerouting through Specctra. The first exchange misrepresented overlapping priority zones, so I discarded it and clipped the power-region outlines for the router. I then used local 0.05 mm and 0.025 mm grid searches with selective track and via displacement to close the dense FPGA and translator escapes. After each displacement I checked the affected pin groups and restored candidates that broke another net.

KiCad DRC guided a final prune through 11 checks, from 36 loose ends to zero, while connectivity stayed complete. pcbnew and the exported schematic netlist supplied the all-footprint, all-pad and electrical-equivalence checks. KiCad generated the order artwork, drill data and electrical test file. The 341-second bulk pass gives a reproducible runtime for this board. I did not measure a manual-routing baseline or token savings.
